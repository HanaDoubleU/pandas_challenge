Analysis' Summary
The analysis consisted of 9 parts. The 1st and 2nd parts entailed getting a general idea of the data by generating statistical values like sums, means, and percentages from the data, and viewing them through 2 lenses: (1) each district and (2) each school. Then, the 3rd and 4th parts examined the maximums and minimums among the aforementioned percentages, which was facilitated by sorting and ordering the data into new dataframes. Finally, the last parts of this analysis prepared the data to reveal any influence which the schools' scores may be experiencing because of other variables; such as grade, school spending, school size and school type.
Conclusions and Comparisons
Comparing percentages in % Overall Passing demonstrates that highest-performing schools in the district tend to be charter schools with less students -- regardless of budget -- while lowest-performing schools are usually district schools with more students despite having similar (if not, higher) Per Student Budgets. Comparing scores over each grade shows that the only fluctuation in them seems to be between each school, not the grades themselves, so I conclude that grade has little influence over scores. On the contrary, I conclude that school spending has much influence over passing rates due to the percentages' drastic differences in spending_summary. This is interesting since district schools seemed to have higher Per Student Budget, yet are lowest-performing schools. It leads me to believe that school size is very impactful. What supports this belief of mine is size_summary, whose numbers decline down each row. type_summary cemented my belief: Smaller charter schools /do/ have better school performance.
